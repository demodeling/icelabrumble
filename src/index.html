CREDITS

The fighters are the people of IceLab, the Integrated Science Lab at Umeå University: mathematicians, physicists, ecologists, molecular biologists, plant physiologists and computer scientists sharing one house, one whiteboard wall and a great deal of fika.

Why IceLab is great

Because the interesting problems sit between the disciplines, and IceLab is built for exactly that spot. Flat, non-hierarchical, open — everyone’s opinions are valued, and a question asked over coffee can turn into a project by the end of the week. Multidisciplinary postdoctoral fellows belong to two groups at once, so collaborations start with a person rather than a memo. Group leaders, PhD students, staff scientists and affiliated researchers from half the departments in Umeå argue about models together and come out with better ones.

That is also the whole point of this game. One airborne eDNA read said rhinoceros at 98.7 %, and finding out why takes an ecologist, a statistician, a molecular biologist and somebody who knows what PCR duplicates look like. Here they just take turns kicking it.

Every face is drawn by the game itself: a paper-cutout avatar, not a portrait, and no photograph of anyone is stored or shipped anywhere in this project.

Rounds 1–8 come from the sister game, Swebits Rumble; rounds 9–11 were IceLab’s own.

The rhino is a false positive. No rhinos were harmed; it was one short read that looked like a horse.
